# 02. Transaction Comparables

**Window:** 2021 to present (September 2026).
**Scope:** verification businesses, EPD and LCA data assets, sustainability data platforms, AEC
materials data companies.

**Evidence grade:** every source page behind this document returned 403 at the egress proxy. No
claim here carries `Verified`. Deal values are reported only where a public source stated them.
Nothing in this document is estimated, modelled or inferred into a number.

---

## 1. The finding that matters most

**There is no public comparable that prices an asset at EcoSpex's scale.**

Of the twelve core transactions, three disclose a value. All three are large: Intertek and SAI
Global Assurance at AUD 855 million, Arcadis and IBI Group at approximately CAD 873 million, and UL
Solutions and Healthy Buildings International at approximately USD 6 million, which is the smallest
disclosed figure found anywhere in the set.

Every transaction involving a small private LCA, EPD, verification or building-product-data target
was undisclosed. WAP and Coldstream, undisclosed. WAP and Trisight, undisclosed. Bureau Veritas and
Aligned Incentives, a target with EUR 3.5 million of 2023 revenue, undisclosed. Once For All and
2050 Materials, undisclosed. Ecomedes and Mortarr, undisclosed. Material Bank and Amber Engine,
undisclosed. Anguleris and Concora Spec, undisclosed.

Consequence for the mandate: a price expectation cannot be triangulated from this market. It has to
be built bottom-up from billable delivery capacity and the contracted value of the account book. Any
figure presented as a comparable-derived valuation would be invented, and the sell side will be
asked where it came from.

## 4. The pattern

### 4.1 What buyers actually pay for

Across all twenty-one transactions, no stated rationale identifies a dataset or a mapping
methodology as the object of the purchase. The recurring objects are:

1. **Delivery capacity.** Headcount that bills. SLR bought close to 100 WAP employees. Schneider
   bought roughly 400 EcoAct staff.
2. **Client relationships.** SLR cited a blue-chip client base. Material Bank cited its ~500 brand
   partners.
3. **Geography and regulatory positioning.** WAP and Coldstream is the cleanest example in the set:
   a small BC consultancy bought for Canadian presence, an institutional relationship with the
   Athena Sustainable Materials Institute, and readiness for GSA and Federal Buy Clean EPD
   requirements.
4. **Accredited or programme standing.** Intertek bought management systems certification scale.

This is the evidentiary basis for the Phase 1 reframe. The asset should be presented as capacity,
accounts and Canadian positioning, because that is what this market has demonstrably paid for.

### 4.2 What stage targets are at

Operating businesses with revenue, not prototypes. Where revenue is disclosed it is real:
EUR 3.5 million at Aligned Incentives, EUR 70 million at EcoAct. Where headcount is disclosed it is
substantial: ~100 at WAP, ~400 at EcoAct.

The single venture-stage target in the set, 2050 Materials, went to a strategic that wanted a
product to plug into an existing data infrastructure. That is the exception, and it is worth noting
that 2050 Materials was a shipped platform with API partnerships, not a methodology.

There is no comparable in which a buyer acquired a prototype or a partially built platform. The
software modules in this asset are unlikely to carry value in a transaction of this shape.

### 4.3 Structure: the question cannot be answered from comparables

**This is a recorded absence and it is a real limitation on the deal-shape decision.**

A search across the twelve core comparables returned no public statement of whether any small
private target was acquired by asset purchase or share purchase. Announcements say "acquired" and
stop. Structure was visible in exactly two places, and both are the wrong shape to learn from:

- **Arcadis and IBI Group**, where the target was a Canadian listed company and the per-share
  consideration and premium were disclosed as a matter of securities law.
- **Verisk and 3E**, where the earnout and deferred payment structure was disclosed because the
  seller was a US public company.

Neither tells you how a private Canadian verification service gets bought.

The practical consequence: the asset-versus-share decision has to be made on the chain-of-title
exposure and on what the buyer needs to carry forward, not on precedent. Given that chain of title
is unresolved across the register and that a period of third-party access without a signed agreement
sits behind it, the asset purchase is the structurally safer default. That is a risk judgment, and
it is not a comparables-derived conclusion. It should not be presented as one.

### 4.4 The counterparty is usually a serial acquirer

Three of the buyers in this set are repeat small-cap acquirers rather than one-off strategics:

- **WAP Sustainability:** Trisight (Sep 2022), Coldstream (Dec 2022), then acquired by SLR (Jan
  2026). A roll-up that got rolled up.
- **Anguleris:** Modlar (2023), ClubDesign (2024), Concora Spec (Feb 2025).
- **Cority:** WeSustain (2021), Reporting 21 (2022), Greenstone (2023).

A first-time acquirer has to build a process. A serial acquirer has one, and will move faster and
diligence more narrowly. Phase 3 should weight demonstrated repeat acquisition behaviour, and the
scorecard's acquisition currency dimension should read acquisition *history* at small scale, not
balance sheet size. A firm with USD 5 billion of revenue that has never bought a CAD 2 million
business is not a buyer.

## 5. What is missing from this set

Searches that were run and returned nothing, recorded because absence is a finding:

- **No acquisition of Green Badger**, a LEED documentation software vendor, was found.
- **No acquisition of or by an EPD programme operator** (SCS Global Services, ASTM International)
  was found in 2024 or 2025. Programme operator standing appears not to trade in this window.
- **No One Click LCA acquisition of EPD Hub** was found. The visible relationship is product
  category rule alignment, not ownership.
- **No transaction was found in which the acquired object was a standards-mapping dataset or
  methodology.** This is the most important absence in the document, and it is consistent with the
  Phase 1 finding that the rating bodies are converting that mapping into public infrastructure.
