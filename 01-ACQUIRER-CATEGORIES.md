## 1. The headline, before the categories

**The seed hypothesis was that TIC firms are the strongest fit because the asset is a manual
verification service with proprietary methodology. The category is right. The reason is wrong, and
the difference decides who gets approached.**

TIC firms will not buy this to acquire a capability they lack. They already run green building
product verification at scale. Intertek operates a Building Products Environmental Certification
Program that tests selected sustainable attributes, third-party validates the claims, and then runs
unannounced quarterly plant inspections. NSF is an EPD Program Operator with more than 11,000 active
EPD IDs across more than 40 Product Category Rules. SGS, Kiwa and TUV Rheinland each publish EPD and
LCA service lines. CSA Group operates an EPD programme and a Sustainability Mark.

What a TIC firm or a consultancy would buy is delivery capacity, manufacturer accounts, and a
Canadian footprint. That is a materially different sale. It prices on billable capability and
transferable relationships, and it does not require the methodology to be defensible.

Three findings force that reframe.

### Finding 1. The multi-standard mapping layer is being commoditized by the standard owners

This is the most consequential thing in this document.

The Common Materials Framework, curated by mindful MATERIALS, translates the industry's
certifications, disclosures and product data into a consistent comparable format. At the 2026
Greenbuild conference, USGBC, IWBI, ILFI, BRE and AIA acknowledged a collective effort to align
their rating systems and climate action programs around the CMF, and the CMF is being incorporated
into LEED v5, WELL v2 and LBC 5.0. The Health Product Declaration Collaborative planned to ship a
CMF Data Module inside the HPD builder by early 2026, exposing manufacturer data through HPDC's
established APIs.

The cross-walk between LEED, WELL, Living Building Challenge and BREEAM is the core of the EcoSpex
verification matrix. That cross-walk is becoming a free, open framework maintained by the rating
bodies themselves, on a published timetable, with an API.

Set against that: the founder marked 21 of 38 register rows Low replication difficulty and only 5
High. The sell side's own assessment and the market's direction point the same way. The retired
claims about AI and about 100 percent accuracy are excluded from this reasoning entirely, and
excluding them costs the argument nothing, because the external evidence is sufficient on its own.

**Re-tested 3 September 2026 (Amendment 1). The finding holds on its load-bearing leg.**
BuildingGreen, independent of mindful MATERIALS, describes LEED v5 as scoring products on a matrix
modelled on the CMF and reconceiving multi-attribute assessment by leveraging it. That is CMF inside
a published credit structure, and LEED v5 is mandatory for new registrations from 1 July 2026. The
collaboration also dates from 2023, so this is a three-year programme that has landed, not a 2026
announcement that might slip. One supporting leg is withdrawn: the HPDC CMF Data Module ship claim
did not corroborate. It is replaced by a harder fact, that the CMF Portal is powered by Ecomedes.

**Implication: the dataset and the mapping logic are not what anyone will pay for. The service, the
accounts and the geography are.**

**Amendment 2 removes two of those three.** The account base is pipeline, not contracted revenue.
What remains that is unambiguously saleable is PEL's actual trailing revenue, the brand, and the
Ontario institutional relationships. See `00-SCOPE-AND-GROUND-TRUTH.md` section 1B.

### Finding 2. The distribution layer has no unfilled gap this asset closes

- EC3 is free and open-access, with Autodesk, Bentley, Ecomedes, 2050 Materials, Tangible and
  cove.tool consuming its EPD data via API.
- Acelab's Material Hub is described as integrating embodied carbon data from EC3 and pulling HPD
  disclosures directly from HPDC, with sustainability data verified at the source, across more than
  250,000 products from more than 10,000 brands and more than 2,000 certifications.
- Sustainable Minds' Transparency Catalog claims coverage of every North American manufacturer
  creating EPDs or ingredient disclosures, from every program operator, across more than 2,100
  manufacturers.
- Deltek Specpoint, which hosts AIA MasterSpec, already ships sustainability language and
  requirements for LEED v4.0 and v4.1, CalGreen, Green Globes and the IgCC / ASHRAE 189.1, with
  ongoing updates.

The July 2026 brief's wedge, that Acelab lacks a verified data feed, is narrower than stated. The
Acelab material dated May 2025 describes EC3 and HPDC integrations already in place. Acelab's
absence from one Building Transparency partner list is not evidence that no integration exists, and
that tension is carried unresolved in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` rather than argued in either direction.

### Finding 3. The one repeating counterparty shape is the small tuck-in for geography and accounts

WAP Sustainability acquired Trisight Engineering in September 2022 and Coldstream Consulting of
Revelstoke, British Columbia in December 2022. The stated rationale for Coldstream was a Canadian
presence through Coldstream's relationship with the Athena Sustainable Materials Institute,
experience in concrete and wood, and positioning for GSA and Federal Buy Clean EPD requirements.
SLR then acquired WAP effective 13 January 2026, bringing close to 100 employees.

That chain is the closest thing in the market to a template for this asset: a small Canadian
verification consultancy bought for geography, relationships and regulatory positioning, inside a
roll-up that was itself later bought. Nothing in the comparable set states that a dataset or a
methodology was the object of a purchase. See `02-TRANSACTION-COMPS.md`.

## 3. Categories in detail

### A. Testing, Inspection and Certification firms and their roll-ups

**Theory as seeded:** the asset is a manual verification service with proprietary methodology, which
is the standard TIC bolt-on shape.

**Theory as revised:** correct category, wrong mechanism. TIC firms do not lack this capability.
They buy small verification businesses to add billable delivery capacity, named manufacturer
accounts, and geographic coverage in a market where a regulation has just created demand. The asset
qualifies on capacity, accounts and geography. It does not qualify on capability scarcity, and any
approach built on capability scarcity will fail its first diligence question.

**Candidates (18):** Intertek, SGS, Bureau Veritas, UL Solutions, TUV Rheinland, TUV SUD, DNV, Kiwa,
Element Materials Technology, Applus+, Eurofins, QIMA, NSF, SCS Global Services, CSA Group,
International Code Council / ICC-ES, BSI, RINA.

**Timing.** Mixed and mostly unhelpful. Bureau Veritas filled its product LCA gap with Aligned
Incentives in October 2024. SLR filled its product sustainability gap with WAP in January 2026.
Intertek's large assurance move was SAI Global Assurance in 2021 and its building products
environmental programme predates that. The firms with an unfilled Canadian green-building-product
delivery gap are the interesting subset, not the firms with an unfilled capability gap.

**Deal shape.** Asset purchase for the service, methodology and accounts. Share purchase only where
the buyer specifically wants the Canadian entity and its institutional relationships, which
reintroduces full chain-of-title exposure.

**Falsifier.** If a buyer in this category states that it prices tuck-ins on defensible IP rather
than on billable capacity and client relationships, the category collapses to nothing, because the
asset cannot win an IP defensibility argument. Nothing found so far suggests they do.

**Amendment 3 constraint.** Delivery capacity is two people. Every comparable in this space bought
tens or hundreds of billable staff. A TIC firm buying "capacity" here is buying one person who must
agree to transfer and stay, plus a second who is in an unresolved payment dispute and holds no
signed IP assignment. That is not a capacity acquisition and it should not be presented as one. The
category survives, but what it would be buying is the dataset, the brand and the Ontario
relationships, with the founder on a retention arrangement. Price accordingly and expect the buyer
to structure most of the consideration as earnout or retention.

**Verdict: confirmed as a category, reframed twice. Carry to Phase 3 at the highest weight, but
scored against a retention-and-assets transaction, not a capacity tuck-in.**

### B. Engineering and environmental consultancies

**Theory:** these firms buy capability plus footprint, and they buy small. The Canadian federal
Standard on Embodied Carbon in Construction created a disclosure obligation on suppliers to federal
projects: ready-mixed concrete from 31 December 2022 on projects at CAD 5 million or more using 100
cubic metres or more, with at least a 10 percent reduction against the regional average, then
structural and reinforcing steel and whole-building LCA from 1 September 2025. LEED v5 became
mandatory for new registrations from 1 July 2026 with embodied carbon quantification as a
prerequisite for all projects. A consultancy with Canadian building-products clients and no
Canadian product-verification bench has a resourcing problem right now.

**Candidates (18):** SLR Consulting, WSP, Stantec, Arcadis, Tetra Tech, ERM, Ramboll, AECOM, Jacobs,
GHD, Wood, Introba, Buro Happold, Salas O'Brien, Dillon Consulting, RWDI, Englobe, Pinchin.

**Timing.** SLR is out; it filled the gap with WAP in January 2026 and then had WAP announce the
Ecomedes distribution partnership in June 2026. Arcadis bought Canadian scale in IBI Group in 2022
but that was multidisciplinary design, not product verification. The unfilled-gap subset is the
Canadian-heavy mid-tier.

**Deal shape.** Asset purchase, tuck-in. Consultancies absorb teams and books of business.

**Falsifier, now triggered.** This document originally said that if the ~60 accounts turned out to be
pipeline rather than contracted recurring revenue, this category would have nothing to buy, because a
consultancy tuck-in is priced on the book. Amendment 2 states they are pipeline. The falsifier has
fired and it must be honoured rather than argued around.

What survives is narrower and it is not nothing: PEL's actual trailing revenue since 2014, whatever
that proves to be, plus a Canadian delivery relationship in a market where the federal embodied
carbon standard extended to steel and whole-building LCA on 1 September 2025. A consultancy with
Canadian building-products clients and no product-verification bench still has a resourcing problem.
But it is now a two-person answer to that problem, which is a small answer.

**Verdict: downgraded from confirmed to conditional. Carry to Phase 3 at reduced weight, and hold
the weighting open until PEL trailing 24 month revenue is known. If that number is small, this
category closes.**

### C. EPD programme operators and certifiers

**Theory:** their economics are per-document verification, which is structurally the same shape as
PEL. A programme operator adding a multi-attribute listing product alongside EPD verification would
be extending an existing revenue motion rather than entering a new one.

**Candidates (9):** UL Solutions, NSF, SCS Global Services, ASTM International, International EPD
System, IBU, EPD Hub, Smart EPD, CSA Group. Five are new to this list; four already appear under A.

**Why it is weak.** Programme operator standing is accredited and is the thing of value; it is not
transferable by acquiring an unaccredited service. A search for acquisitions of or by EPD programme
operators in 2024 and 2025 returned no transaction, which suggests this standing does not trade.
Recorded as an absence, not as proof of impossibility.

**Deal shape.** Asset purchase of the service and accounts only.

**Falsifier.** If any programme operator has acquired a non-accredited verification service in the
last three years, the category strengthens sharply. None was found.

**Verdict: partial. Carry to Phase 3 at low weight, narrowed to CSA Group and NSF for the North
American building-products overlap.**

### D. Specifier and materials platforms

**Theory as seeded:** they need a verification or data layer.

**Rejected.** They have one, and where they do not, it is free. EC3 is free and open-access with a
published API partner list. HPDC exposes disclosures through established APIs. The CMF gives them
the cross-walk. Deltek Specpoint already ships LEED v4.0 and v4.1, CalGreen, Green Globes and
IgCC / ASHRAE 189.1 content natively. Acelab is described as pulling EC3 and HPDC data at source
across more than 250,000 products.

**Candidates (11):** Acelab, Material Bank, Anguleris (BIMsmith, Swatchbox, Modlar, Concora Spec),
Ecomedes, Sustainable Minds Transparency Catalog, Deltek Specpoint, RIB Software (SpecLink), NBS,
ARCAT, CADdetails, Unifi Labs.

**Timing.** Ecomedes is the clearest case of the timing inversion. It filled its verified-data gap
via the WAP partnership announced 22 June 2026, three months before this analysis. It is a poorly
timed acquirer and should rank low, not high, precisely because it is the most obvious name.

**Amendment 1 closes this further.** HPD Collaborative states that it connects HPD data from more
than 700 manufacturers across more than 10,000 published HPD reports with the CMF Portal, powered by
Ecomedes. Ecomedes is not merely a platform that filled its gap. It is the technology partner behind
the rating bodies' own shared cross-walk portal. The incumbent operator of the thing this asset
proposes to sell is already in place and already selected.

**Verdict: rejected as acquirers, with higher confidence than at first writing. Retained for Phase 4
as channel.**

### E. Building product data aggregators and manufacturer marketing platforms

**Theory:** the manufacturer-facing side of the same market, and a proven home for small
acquisitions.

**Rejected as acquirers of this asset,** although the category contains the most active small-cap
buyer found. Anguleris acquired Modlar in 2023, ClubDesign in 2024 and Concora Spec in February
2025. But a search for an Anguleris EPD or sustainability data strategy returned nothing; its
published sustainability positioning concerns sample waste reduction. Material Bank's data
acquisition was Amber Engine, a PIM platform, in March 2022. These buyers acquire reach, samples and
BIM content, not verification services.

**Candidates (9), 2 new:** Ecomedes, Anguleris, Material Bank, ARCAT, CADdetails, Sweets / CMD,
4specs, Concora Spec, Amber Engine.

**Falsifier.** If Anguleris or Material Bank publicly signals demand for third-party environmental
verification, this moves to confirmed immediately. Worth a standing watch.

**Verdict: rejected as acquirers. Retained for Phase 4 as channel.**

### F. LCA and embodied carbon software

**Rejected.** These vendors hold orders of magnitude more data and the asset adds nothing on volume.
One Click LCA has been buying adjacent capability, taking ENVI-met in September 2024, not data
assets. 2050 Materials was itself acquired by Once For All in November 2025 and is no longer a
buyer. Building Transparency is a non-profit giving its data away.

**Candidates (10):** One Click LCA, cove.tool, Building Transparency, 2050 Materials (Once For All),
Ecochain, Sphera, Tangible, Emidat, Athena Sustainable Materials Institute, Sustainable Minds.

**Falsifier.** The asset would have to demonstrate coverage these vendors lack, in a segment they
want. Multi-attribute non-carbon compliance in Canada is the only plausible version of that, and it
is unproven.

**Verdict: rejected.**

### G. BIM and design ecosystem incumbents

**Rejected on scale and on timing.** Autodesk and Bentley already consume EC3 data by API and
Building Transparency publishes an EC3 integration for Autodesk Construction Cloud. These companies
do not transact at this size for this kind of asset.

**Candidates (7):** Autodesk, Nemetschek Group, Trimble, Bentley Systems, Hexagon, Procore, RIB
Software.

**Verdict: rejected.**

### H. ESG and supply chain data companies

**Rejected on timing and on buyer persona.** The three most plausible names have each just filled
the adjacent gap: Sphera acquired SupplyShift in January 2024, Cority acquired WeSustain in 2021,
Reporting 21 in 2022 and Greenstone in 2023, and Assent, Ottawa-based and backed by a USD 350M Vista
Equity investment, acquired iPoint in July 2026 in what was described as its first acquisition,
adding lifecycle assessment and product stewardship.

Beyond timing, the buyer persona is wrong. These companies sell to a manufacturer's compliance
function against regulatory reporting obligations. PEL and GBM sell to a manufacturer's marketing
function and to specifiers. The revenue motions do not resemble each other.

**Candidates (11):** Sphera, Assent, EcoVadis, Watershed, Persefoni, Novisto, Verisk 3E, Source
Intelligence, Cority, IntegrityNext, Benchmark Gensuite.

**Verdict: rejected.**

### I. Canadian strategics

**Theory:** the one category where the regulatory position is the asset rather than a footnote, and
the one where the timing inversion bites least.

CSA Group is the standout. It is an independent Canadian not-for-profit doing global product testing
and certification, it operates an EPD programme, it issued a first EPD for the Cement Association of
Canada, it operates a Sustainability Mark, and it has a published alliance with the Canada Green
Building Council supporting LEED v4 and its own EPD programme. CAGBC is proposing Canadian Regional
Alternative Compliance Paths into LEED v5 with more expected through 2026. The federal embodied
carbon standard is live and expanding. A Canadian certification body with a green building alliance,
facing a regulation that just extended to steel and whole-building LCA, has a demand problem that
manual delivery capacity solves.

**Candidates (12), 2 new:** CSA Group, Assent, Cority, Novisto, Stantec, WSP, Englobe, Pinchin,
Dillon Consulting, RWDI, QAI Laboratories, Athena Sustainable Materials Institute. CAGBC is noted as
an ecosystem body, not a buyer.

**Timing.** Assent and Cority are out on the inversion. CSA Group, QAI and the Canadian mid-tier
consultancies show no filled gap.

**Deal shape.** Either. CSA Group's not-for-profit structure may favour an asset purchase; a
Canadian consultancy tuck-in would likely also be an asset purchase.

**Falsifier.** If the IESO subsidy relationship that EcoSpex states underpins its pilot pricing is a
one-time programme rather than a standing relationship, the "institutional position" thins to a
grant history and the category weakens considerably.

**Verdict: confirmed. Carry to Phase 3 at high weight.**

### J. Rating system owners and their commercial arms

**Added beyond the seeds, and rejected. This is the most important rejection in the document.**

These organizations are not buyers of a mapping of their own content. They are the reason that
mapping is losing value. USGBC, IWBI, ILFI, BRE and AIA aligned around the CMF at Greenbuild 2026;
CMF is entering LEED v5, WELL v2 and LBC 5.0; HPDC is shipping a CMF data module with existing APIs.
They are converting the cross-walk into public infrastructure.

**Candidates (10):** USGBC / GBCI, CAGBC, IWBI, ILFI, BRE / BREEAM, PHIUS, Passive House Institute,
AIA, HPD Collaborative, mindful MATERIALS.

**Verdict: rejected, and treated as the clock on the whole thesis.**

### K. Manufacturer corporate venture arms

**Added beyond the seeds, and rejected.** The precedent is real but it is the wrong instrument:
Saint-Gobain NOVA seeded Ecomedes with USD 1.5M alongside PivotNorth in September 2020 and
participated in a USD 3.25M round led by Microsoft's M12 in March 2021. Those are minority venture
investments in a platform, not acquisitions of a verification service.

There is also a structural conflict. A building product manufacturer owning the body that verifies
building products destroys the independence that is the service's entire premise.

**Candidates (11):** Saint-Gobain NOVA, Holcim MAQER Ventures, CRH Ventures, Cemex Ventures, Sika,
Owens Corning, Kingspan, Mohawk Industries, Interface, Armstrong World Industries, Masco.

**Verdict: rejected as acquirers. A minority investment is a different conversation and is out of
scope for this brief.**

### L. Public-sector procurement and Buy Clean compliance vendors

**Added beyond the seeds, and reclassified rather than rejected.**

The demand is real and dated: Canada's Standard on Embodied Carbon in Construction under the
Treasury Board Policy on Green Procurement, concrete from 31 December 2022, steel and whole-building
LCA from 1 September 2025. WAP explicitly cited GSA and Federal Buy Clean EPD requirements when
buying Coldstream.

But searching for organizations whose business is Buy Clean compliance surfaced almost no distinct
names. The work is done by the consultancies and programme operators already counted in A, B, C and
I. Only the Cement Association of Canada is new, and it is an industry association, not a buyer.

**Verdict: reclassified. This is the trigger that makes categories A, B and I act. It is not a
category of buyer, and treating it as one would have produced a list of six names that are already
on the list.**

## 4. What this means for the approach

**Phase 3 screens before it profiles.** Section 4.4 of `02-TRANSACTION-COMPS.md` concludes that a
firm with billions in revenue that has never bought a CAD 2 million business is not a buyer. That is
a filter, not a scoring dimension, and applying it after profiling wastes the effort it is supposed
to save. Two screens run in sequence against the ~48 organizations in categories A, B, C (narrowed)
and I:

1. **Demonstrated acquisition at or below roughly CAD 5 million.** No evidence of a small
   acquisition means no profile. Being large is not being a buyer.
2. **No gap-filling event in the last 24 months.** The timing table in `02-TRANSACTION-COMPS.md`
   section 4.5 already removes eight names on this test.

Expected survivors: 15 to 20. The profiles get better because the effort concentrates, and the
screen itself is a finding, because a category that loses most of its members to screen 1 was never
a real category.

Three consequences worth deciding on before Phase 3 runs:

1. **The pitch is not IP, and after Amendment 2 it is not accounts either.** Any approach leading
   with proprietary methodology invites the one diligence question the asset cannot survive, and the
   founder's own Low replication marks sit in the register waiting to be found. Any approach leading
   with the account base invites a request for the contract list, which is pipeline. What is left to
   lead with is PEL's trailing revenue and the Ontario position, and neither is quantified yet.
2. **The window is closing and it is measurable.** CMF entered the LEED v5 credit structure and LEED
   v5 became mandatory for new registrations on 1 July 2026. These are dated events, not trends.
   Each quarter of delay reduces what the mapping logic is worth.
3. **The deal-shape question cannot be settled from comparables.** No public statement of structure
   was found for any small private target in the comparable set. It will have to be decided on the
   chain-of-title exposure rather than on precedent. See `02-TRANSACTION-COMPS.md`.
4. **Nothing should be approached until the James position is resolved.** The unresolved payment
   dispute with the core technical contributor and the absence of any chain-of-title record are one
   risk, not two. See `00-SCOPE-AND-GROUND-TRUTH.md` section 1C. This is a sequencing constraint on
   Phase 5 and it binds regardless of which target ranks first.
5. **A sale may not be the right instrument, and Phase 3 should be allowed to conclude that.** At
   two FTE with a pipeline rather than a book, the honest candidate outcomes are an asset purchase at
   a low number for the data, brand and Ontario relationships with the founder on a consulting
   agreement, or no sale and a licensing or channel arrangement instead. Phase 3 should not be
   constructed so that ranking targets is the only permitted output.
